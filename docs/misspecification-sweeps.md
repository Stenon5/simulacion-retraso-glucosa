# Misspecification sweeps

The baseline simulation generates data with the same model the estimator
assumes. Under those conditions the estimator is being asked to solve its own
equations, so recovering the delay is a verification and not a validation.
This document is the plan for breaking that assumption on purpose.

Written before any sweep has been run. Nothing here has results.

## Why this comes before anything else

Moving to richer glucose curves from an open source simulator changes where
the blood glucose signal comes from. It does not change the part that is
circular, which is the tissue model. A simulator can supply a realistic blood
glucose trace and the tissue signals would still be generated by first order
diffusion with constant delay and white noise. The sweeps below attack the
tissue model itself, which is the actual assumption under test.

## The sweeps

Each sweep changes one assumption at a time, with everything else at the
baseline. The deliverable for each one is the point where the estimator stops
meeting the criteria in `thresholds.yaml`.

### 1. Variable delay

The time constants drift over the record instead of staying fixed, for example
slowly, or with a step change standing in for a change in perfusion.

Report: the bias and spread as a function of how fast the delay moves, and
whether a sliding window version of the estimator recovers anything useful.
Expected to break first, because the estimator fits one constant per depth
over the whole window.

### 2. Noise correlated between depths

The two depth readings come from one instrument, so part of their noise is
shared. Sweep the correlation between the two noise series from zero up to
strongly correlated.

Report: the bias as a function of correlation. This one directly attacks the
error in variables correction, which assumes independent noise, so the
correction may make things worse rather than better. That is the result to
look for.

### 3. Sensor drift

Add a slow baseline drift to each depth, independent or shared, on top of the
white noise. Optical sensors drift, and the noise estimator in `docs/spec.md`
reads the second difference, which is blind to slow drift.

Report: the bias as a function of drift rate, and whether the noise estimate
stays correct while the delay estimate goes wrong, which would be the quiet
failure mode.

### 4. Kinetics that are not first order

Generate the tissue signals with something the estimator does not assume. Two
options, both worth running: a transport delay plus first order diffusion, and
a second order response. Both are still simple, and both are wrong in a
different direction.

Report: the bias, and whether the condition number or any other quantity the
estimator computes gives any warning that the model is wrong. An estimator
that fails loudly is much more useful than one that fails quietly.

### 5. Sampling interval and record length

Sweep how often the sensor reads and how long the record is.

Report: the shortest record that still identifies both time constants at the
baseline. This sets what a device would have to collect before saying
anything.

### 6. Separation between the two depths

Sweep how different the two time constants are, from nearly identical to far
apart.

Report: the separation below which the problem stops being identifiable. This
is a hardware requirement in disguise, because the separation is set by where
the two depths sit.

## How a sweep is reported

* One row per grid cell, with mean bias and standard deviation side by side,
  never the mean alone.
* The threshold from `thresholds.yaml` that the cell fails, named explicitly.
* The seed and configuration for every row.
* A plain sentence saying where the estimator stopped working, in units a
  reader can act on.

## What a failed sweep means

It means the method has a stated domain of validity instead of an implied one.
A sweep that shows the estimator breaking under correlated noise is a more
useful contribution than another run where it succeeds under its own
assumptions.
